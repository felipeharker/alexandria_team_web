The Screen

Controls on the left, the preview on the right.

  1. Region. The panel's width, height and units. The SVG comes out at this size.
  2. Grid. Square, Hex or Triangle cells.
  3. Cell Size, Edge Cells. Cell Size is the length of a side. Whole keeps the cells that fit; Clipped fills the region and cuts the edge cells at its boundary.
  4. Scale. Each cell is scaled about its centre, from Min at the nearest attractor to Max at the Reach distance and beyond. Empty Reach means the farthest cell. Largest at the Attractor swaps them.
  5. Attractors. Points and curves, as many as you need. Add Point and Add Curve make your next click start a new one. Each is listed here: click one to select it, or × to remove it. The selected point's X and Y can be typed, from the region's bottom left corner; a curve is drawn Straight or Smooth.
  6. Download SVG. Saves the linework, named after the file name at the top. Copy SVG copies its code instead.
  7. The attractors. A click moves the selected point, or adds a point to the selected curve. Click another attractor to select it. On a curve, click the line to insert a point, drag to move one, double-click to remove one; double-click a point attractor to remove it.
  8. The region. Its boundary and every cell, as the SVG will have them. The pointer's X and Y show at the top right.

Make a Grid

The usual order. The preview updates as you go.

  1. Set the RegionThe panel's size, in the units it will be made in.
  2. Choose the GridSquare, Hex or Triangle, the cell size, and Whole or Clipped.
  3. Place the AttractorsClick the preview to move the point. Add Point or Add Curve for more, and click out a curve's points.
  4. Set the ScaleMin and Max for the smallest and largest cells, and Reach for how far the change spreads.
  5. Cull, If WantedRandom Cull takes a share of the farthest cells out at random, like Grasshopper's Random Reduce.
  6. Download SVGInto the project's folder, then import it into Rhino, CorelDRAW or Illustrator.

Good to Know

  • Full size. The SVG is in the region's units, so it imports at its real size. Feet come out as inches and meters as centimeters, since SVG has neither.
  • Several attractors. Each cell takes its size from the attractor nearest to it, so two points make two pools of small cells, and a point beside a curve pulls the cells toward whichever is closer.
  • Clean linework. One closed shape per cell and the boundary, with no fills, in groups named Region and Cells. Tick Include the Attractors in the SVG to add them too, in a group named Attractors.
  • Random Cull. Both run from 0 to 1. Start leaves the cells nearest the attractors alone: at 0.33, the cull works on the farthest 67%. Cull is the share of those taken out at random. The same Seed always takes out the same cells, and they stay put while you move the attractors; change it for another pick.
  • Clipped edges. A clipped cell's cut side lies on the boundary, so that line is there twice: delete one before cutting.
  • Settings stay. Everything, the attractors too, is remembered in this browser.

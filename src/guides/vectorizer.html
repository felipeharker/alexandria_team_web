The Screen

Controls on the left, the preview on the right.

  1. Image. Choose one, drop it on the page, or paste it with Ctrl+V. PNG, JPG, WebP or GIF. It stays in your browser: nothing is uploaded.
  2. Colors. How many colors to look for. The in-between shades along edges are left out, so you may get fewer.
  3. The color list. Ticked colors are kept. Untick one to merge it into the nearest kept color. Omit leaves a color empty, like the white background here.
  4. Keep a Color, Omit a Color. Press one, then click the image: the nearest color in the list is kept or omitted, or a new one is added.
  5. Precision. 1 gives fewer, smoother curves and ignores specks; 10 follows the image closely and keeps small detail. Clean Geometry, under it, makes nearly level and plumb edges exact and near-circles true circles: tick it for artwork drawn with a ruler and compass, leave it off for slanted or hand-drawn shapes.
  6. Download SVG. Saves the SVG, named after the file name at the top. Copy SVG copies its code instead.
  7. Vector, Original, Outlines. The trace, the image it came from, or the trace's outlines over a faint fill. Scroll to zoom, drag to pan.
  8. The checkerboard. Where nothing is drawn: omitted colors and transparent parts of the image.

Trace an Image

The usual order.

  1. Open the ImageThe largest, cleanest file the client has: a small or heavily compressed JPG traces poorly.
  2. Omit the BackgroundPress Omit on the background's color, often white, so only the artwork is left.
  3. Check the ColorsUntick extras, or raise Colors if one is missing. Keep a Color adds one you click on.
  4. Set the PrecisionZoom in and switch to Outlines: lower it if the curves are lumpy, raise it if detail is lost. Tick Clean Geometry for squared-up, compass-drawn artwork.
  5. Download SVGInto the project's folder, then import it into Rhino or CorelDRAW and scale it to size.

Good to Know

  • One layer per color. Each color is one compound path, and neighbouring colors share the same border, with no gaps or overlaps, so each can be cut as its own layer.
  • Sharp corners, straight edges. Corners stay sharp and straight edges come out as true lines at every Precision. Edges land where the image's own soft edges put them, to within a small fraction of a pixel, even on a small logo, and thin lines and outlines keep their width.
  • Pixel size. The SVG is the image's size in pixels. Scale it to the real size where you place it.
  • Text stays shapes. Letters are traced as outlines, not type: retype them in the right font if they'll be edited.
  • Settings stay. Colors, Precision and Clean Geometry are remembered in this browser for the next image.

A Color and Its Matches

One card per color: the color on the left, its three nearest matches from each library on the right.

  1. Swatch. Click it to pick a color by eye.
  2. Name and Hex. Type a hex code, with or without the #; the matches follow as you type.
  3. Best Match. The nearest color in each library comes first, then the next two.
  4. Match %. How alike the two look, with a word for it.
  5. Formula on File. The office has a Matthews Paint formula for that color. Click it to open it in the Color Library.

Reading the Match

Match = 100% less 2.5% for every unit of ΔE (CIEDE2000), the industry measure of how different two colors look.

MatchΔEWhat It Means
98% and up1 or lessIndistinguishable. The same color to the eye.
95%up to 2Very Close. A difference only side by side.
90%up to 4Close. Fine for most signs; check it against the brand guide.
80%up to 8Noticeable. Visibly different; look for a better match or mix one.
Below 80%over 8Different. Not a match.

Sharing a List

A CSV is the list: export it, keep it with the project, and anyone can import it.

The Export Has a Column For

  • Each color's Name and Hex.
  • Its best Sherwin-Williams, Pantone and 3M 3630 match: code, name, hex and match %.
  • Any Formula on File, with its Matthews number and product line.

Good to Know

  • Screens aren't paint. Every swatch is an on-screen approximation. Check the match against a physical chip, a printed guide or a drawdown before ordering.
  • 3M's colors are shown unlit. A translucent film looks brighter on a lit sign face; 3M's matching Pantone is the better guide for that.
  • The list lives in this browser. Another browser or computer starts empty; export a CSV to keep it.
  • From a formula to its matches. In the Color Library, Find Matches on any formula adds its color here.

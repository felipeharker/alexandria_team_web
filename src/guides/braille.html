The Braille

Six places per cell; the dark ones are raised.

  1. The Capital Sign. A cell of its own before a capital letter. Braille only capitalizes the start of a sentence, names and acronyms, so type Restroom, not RESTROOM.
  2. A Cell. Two columns of three places: dots 1 to 3 down the left, 4 to 6 down the right. The SVG has a circle for each dark one only.
  3. The Spacing. Centre to centre: dots in a cell, cells along a line, and lines below each other.

Make the Braille

The usual order.

  1. Type the TextThe sign's words, as they should read. Each line of text is a line of braille.
  2. Grade 2 for ADAThe tool starts on Grade 2, contracted, as ADA signs need. Grade 1 spells every word out.
  3. Check the DimensionsThey start at the middle of the ADA's ranges. A value outside them is flagged.
  4. Download SVGImport it at full size and place it below the raised text.

Good to Know

  • Proof it. Grade 2 comes from liblouis, the braille translator screen readers use. The Braille Characters under the preview can be pasted into a braille translator to read back, or checked by whoever proofs the signs.
  • Braille from elsewhere. A line of braille characters, pasted in, is used exactly as it is.
  • Numbers. A number sign starts every number, so Room 101 needs no extra work. A letter after a number, as in 200B, is handled too.
  • Unusual characters? One the tool has no braille sign for is named in red under the text: check the braille for it.
  • Where it goes. Braille sits below the raised text, at least 3/8 in from it, and the state or city may add rules: check the project's code.
  • It saves. The text and dimensions stay in this browser.

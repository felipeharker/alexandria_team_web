A sign's text in, its braille out: an SVG of dots at ADA sizes.

Open the Braille Lettering Designer
  1. Text

    Each line of text is a line of braille.

    • Type words as they should read: an all-capitals word gets extra capital signs.
    • Pasted Unicode braille is used cell for cell.
    • The text, grade and dimensions are remembered in this browser.
  2. Braille

    Grade 2 is what ADA signs need. Grade 1 spells every word out.

    Grade 2Red May not translate · check: names characters to check
    Grade 1Red Left out, no braille: names characters with no sign
  3. Dimensions

    Four sizes in inches, center to center, starting at the ADA defaults.

    • A value outside the ADA range turns red, and is still exported.
    • Reset to the ADA Defaults appears once a value differs.
  4. Download SVG · Copy SVG

    The braille as a file, or its code on the clipboard.

    • In inches, one filled circle per raised dot, no margin.
    • The file is named Braille and the first line, such as Braille Restroom.svg.
    • Greyed out with no text, and while Grade 2 loads.
  5. Dark dots raised

    The braille as it will be made.

    • The faint places are not raised, and are not in the SVG.
  6. Braille Characters

    The same braille as selectable text.

    • Copy it into a braille translator to read it back.

Make the Braille

  1. Type the sign's text as it should read
  2. Keep Grade 2 for an ADA sign
  3. Check that no dimension is red
  4. Check the dots, then the characters
  5. Download the SVG and place it on the sign

The Screen

The package's parts on the left, the open page on the right.

  1. The Parts. Document, Details, Page, CMF and Export, in the order of work. Each shows its own controls underneath.
  2. The Tools. Select picks, moves and sizes what's on the page. Dimension, Callout, Text and Shape draw one each.
  3. The Info Box. The page's name, quantity and scope of work, set under Page. It grows to fit a longer scope.
  4. A Drawing. Your own SVG, PDF or image, as large as fits the page's open area. Drag a corner to size it.
  5. A Dimension. Reads X'-X" in red until the drawing has a real size. Type one dimension's real length and the rest fill in to match.
  6. A Callout. A dot on what it points at, one leg, and its text in capitals.
  7. A View Label. A drawing's own title, with a note under it. It goes where the drawing goes.

Make a Package

The usual order.

  1. Fill In the DetailsThe project, the client, the company name and the client's logo. Red on the page is still the template's words.
  2. Add Pages and DrawingsA design page per view. Add Drawing or Image, or drop the files on the page.
  3. Give It a Real SizeClick a red dimension and type its real length, such as 21'-9". Every other dimension on the drawing fills in.
  4. Dimension and Call OutTwo points and a side for a dimension; a point and a place for a callout's text. Add View Label titles a drawing.
  5. List the CMFPaint, vinyl, materials and electrical, each with a code, where it's used and a swatch.
  6. Export PDFExport lists what's unfinished first. Then Save Package File, and keep both with the project.

A Drawing's Real Size

The tool never asks what scale a file was drawn at. One real dimension is enough.

  1. Add the drawing. It lands as large as fits, with no real size. Linework comes with its overall width and height dimensions already drawn, reading X'-X" in red.
  2. Draw any other dimensions you need. They read X'-X" too.
  3. Type one real length. Click a dimension you know, type its length under Real Length, such as 21'-9", and press Enter. The drawing's Overall Width or Overall Height does the same.
  4. The rest fill in. Every other dimension on that drawing now reads its own length, in proportion. Type a different length into any of them later and they all follow again.

  • Size on the page is separate. Drag a corner, or press Fit to Page: the dimensions keep reading the same. Size on the Page can hold a drawing at a standard scale once it has a real size.
  • A red dimension never prints. Export tells you which drawings still have no real size, and leaves their dimensions out of the PDF.

Changing a Drawing's Parts

The pages are the standard's. The design is yours: its colors and lines can be changed here, as in CorelDRAW or Illustrator.

  1. Open the drawing. Click it twice, or press Edit Its Parts. Each shape in it can now be clicked.
  2. Select parts. Click one. Shift-click, or drag a box, for several. Select Same Fill takes every part with that color, to change a whole color at once.
  3. Change them. Fill and Line take any color, or one of the package's CMF swatches. Line Weight is in points, and Opacity fades a part.
  4. Move, restack, delete. Drag the selected parts, or use the arrow keys. Bring to Front and Send to Back restack them, and Delete removes them.
  5. Dimension them. Add Their Dimensions draws the width and height of the box around what's selected: one letter, a logo, a cabinet.
  6. Press Esc when you're done. Or Enter, Done, or a click outside the drawing.
  • Your file isn't touched. The changes belong to this placing of the drawing. Put It Back as Its File Has It clears them all, and Ctrl+Z undoes them one at a time.
  • It has to be all shapes. An SVG or a PDF with live text, pictures, gradients or masks in it can't be opened this way. Save it with its text as outlines (curves) and it can. A picture has no parts: trace it in the Vectorizer first.
  • The changes go everywhere. The PDF, the linework at 1:1 and the dimensions all follow the drawing as you've changed it.

Good to Know

  • Red means not yet. A detail still empty shows the template's words in red, and a dimension with no real size reads X'-X" in red. Neither prints red: a detail prints as a dash, a dimension is left out, and Export tells you first.
  • Click on the drawing's own points. A pink square shows where a click will snap: an end of a line, a corner or a middle. That's what makes a dimension exact.
  • Click twice. A second click on a dimension goes to its length, and on a callout or text to its words. On a drawing it opens the drawing's parts.
  • Lining up. A drawing you move catches on the open area's edges and middle, and on the page's other drawings: a pink line shows what it caught. Hold Alt to move it freely.
  • Several at once. Shift-click, or drag a box over the page, to select several drawings, texts and shapes; Ctrl+A takes them all. Drag one and they all move. Arrange lines them up with each other and evens out the gaps between three or more.
  • Panning. Dragging the background selects, so hold Space and drag to move around the page, or use the middle button. Scroll to zoom.
  • Copies. Ctrl+D duplicates what's selected: a drawing takes its dimensions, callouts and view label along. Ctrl+C and Ctrl+V carry it to another page, and Duplicate Page copies a whole design page.
  • CMF codes in a callout. With a callout or text selected, CMF Code lists the package's colors and materials: pick one and its code, such as P1, goes into the text.
  • Two ways to measure. With the Dimension tool, the bar offers Level or Plumb and Parallel. Level or Plumb measures as a sign's overall width and height are: move above or below the two points for the level distance, left or right for the plumb one. Parallel measures straight from one point to the other, at any angle, for a sloped edge or a diagonal. Shift takes the other of the two, Alt places the line freely, and Measures changes a dimension already drawn.
  • Stacked dimensions. A dimension's line sits in steps of 1/4 in, so two of them land 1/2 in apart, as the standard asks.
  • The same scope on every page. Set it on one page and press Use on Every Page.
  • Futura on the title page. Under Export, Use Futura from This Computer reads Futura Bk BT from your computer, in Chrome or Edge. Until then the company name is in a close stand-in.
  • Undo. Ctrl+Z and Ctrl+Y. Delete removes what's selected, and the arrow keys nudge a drawing.
  • It saves, in this browser. For another computer or another person, Save Package File and open it there.
  • Not for cutting. The fab team cuts from the Fabrication Manager's files in Rhino. Linework from here is for bringing artwork into CAD at its true size.

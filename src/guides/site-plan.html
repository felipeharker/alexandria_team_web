The Screen

Controls on the left, the map on the right.

  1. Address. Type it and press Search. When several places match, they list below it: pick the right one.
  2. Sign Location. The address found, or Placed on the Map after a click, with its coordinates.
  3. Circle Radius. In feet, true to the map's scale: drag, or type up to 5,000.
  4. Highlight Buildings. Press it, then click buildings to darken them, like the one in the circle; click one again to undo. Done when finished.
  5. Street Names, Shadows. Off for the plain figure-ground look; on to name the streets, or to give the buildings soft shadows.
  6. Export PNG. Saves what the map shows, named after the project at the top.
  7. The Circle. Dashed red, with SIGN LOCATION inside. The label grows and shrinks with the circle.

Make a Site Plan

The usual order.

  1. Find the AddressStreet, city and state. The map goes there and places the sign on it.
  2. Place the SignClick the map where the sign really goes: the facade, not the parcel's middle.
  3. Size the CircleBig enough to read at the plan's scale, small enough to point at one building.
  4. Highlight the BuildingIf it helps: darken the client's building, or a whole campus, with Highlight Buildings.
  5. Frame ItZoom and pan until the map shows what the page should: the export is the map as you see it.
  6. Export PNGInto the project's folder, ready to place on a page.

The Map's Look

One office standard for every site plan, kept with the tool.

Land, Parks, WaterVery light flat greys: parks a shade darker than land, water a shade lighter.
BuildingsLight grey footprints with a fine edge. The ones you highlight turn dark grey.
StreetsWhite with a thin grey edge, at their real width, so they keep their size against the buildings as you zoom.
ShadowsOff unless Shadows is on: a soft shadow down and to the right of every building.
LabelsNone, unless Street Names is on: dark grey capitals along the streets.
Sign LocationThe one color: red, as the sign's location on the design package's plans.

Good to Know

  • Check the footprints. The map is OpenStreetMap, which can miss a new building or keep a torn-down one. Compare it with the architect's site plan before it goes in a package.
  • North is up. The map doesn't rotate, so the plan reads like the architect's.
  • Sharp on the page. About 4,800 px across prints at 300 dpi across a 17 x 11 page's drawing area.
  • Keep the credit. The small © OpenStreetMap contributors in the export's corner is what the map's licence asks for. Leave it on the page.
  • Nothing found? Add the city and state, or zoom there yourself and click the map.
  • It saves. The plan stays in this browser. The project name at the top names the PNG.

## Product Lines

Pantone publishes the same colors in several libraries. The letters after the number say which one.

| Library | Suffix | What It Is |
|---|---|---|
| Solid Coated | `C` | The spot ink printed on glossy coated paper. The one brand guides usually give, and the one sign work uses. |
| Solid Uncoated | `U` | The same ink on plain uncoated paper. It looks duller and often lighter. |
| Color Bridge Coated | `CP`, or `PC` on older charts | The closest four-color (CMYK) print of each solid color, beside the solid. For anything printed digitally. |

The three charts under Sources are a Color Bridge chart with each color's CMYK build, a one-page chart of the same numbers for digital proofs, and the Solid Uncoated palette from a printer's artwork guide.

## Reading a Code

- **A number of three or four digits,** `100` to `7547` on these charts, or **a name** for the base inks: Yellow, Orange 021, Warm Red, Red 032, Rubine Red, Rhodamine Red, Purple, Violet, Blue 072, Reflex Blue, Process Blue, Green, Black, and the Cool Grays and Warm Grays 1 to 11.
- **Then the library's letters.** `186 C` and `186 U` are one ink on two papers and don't look alike, so always write the letters.
- **PMS** is the same thing: *PMS 186* means PANTONE 186, with the library left to guess. Ask which.
- Charts shorten the names: *Pro.* is Process, *Wm* Warm, *Cl* Cool, *Rhod* Rhodamine.

## How We Use It

- **A brand color comes in as a Pantone number.** Take the coated (`C`) color unless the client's guide says otherwise.
- **To paint it,** find it in the [Color Library](/colors/library/): the office's Matthews formulas are listed by the color they match, Pantone among them. See [Matthews Paint](/wiki/suppliers/matthews-paint/).
- **To put it in vinyl,** the [3M Films](/wiki/suppliers/3m/) charts print a Pantone number beside the films 3M matched to one.
- **For the nearest of everything,** the [Color Matcher](/colors/) takes a hex code and returns the closest Pantone Solid Coated, Sherwin-Williams and 3M colors.
- **On a drawing,** write it in full, as in *PANTONE 186 C*, beside the paint or vinyl that stands in for it.

## Finishes and Notes

- Pantone colors are inks, so they have no gloss of their own. The paint or film that matches one brings the finish.
- **The CMYK builds are for print.** They come close to the solid color and rarely reach it, bright oranges, greens and blues least of all. Don't choose paint from a CMYK print.
- The Color Bridge chart here dates from 2005. Pantone has added and adjusted colors since, so check a number that isn't on it against a current guide.
- Metallic, pastel and neon Pantone colors aren't on these charts.
- Every swatch below is the chart's color as a screen shows it. Match to a current Pantone guide, never to this page.
